Parliament of Azerbaijan Milli Majlis Bills & Legislation — 14 July 2026

The Milli Majlis tabled a single, unusually large batch of 19 bills on 14 July 2026, touching the Constitutional Law on Normative Legal Acts and amending large parts of the Civil, Criminal, Tax, Labor, Land, Administrative Offenses and Migration Codes in one sitting. This is the broadest omnibus package logged since the Gazette began tracking in June. No new laws were enacted during the reporting period — the most recent enacted law remains No. 441-VIIQD (10 July, customs tariff amendment), one day outside this window.

Nazirlər Kabineti Cabinet of Ministers Decisions & Orders — 14–17 July 2026

The Cabinet issued three Decisions (Nos. 216–218) on 17 July, plus a run of implementation Orders between 14 and 16 July. The 17 July batch is notably diverse — education financing, professional certification, and customs tariff nomenclature in a single day.

DecisionNo. 218 17 Jul 2026

Regulatory Spotlight — Key Themes, Week 29 / 2026

1. The 14 July Omnibus — Broadest Package Tracked to Date

Nineteen bills tabled in one sitting, spanning the Civil, Criminal, Tax, Labor, Land, Administrative Offenses and Migration Codes plus a new Crowdfunding law and Constitutional Law on Normative Acts amendment. The sheer breadth suggests a coordinated legislative push ahead of the autumn session. Corporate, tax, employment, and land-use practices should each nominate an owner to track the relevant sub-package through committee review — the Land Code changes (items 12–13) and the Tax/Migration/Procurement package (item 6) look most commercially consequential.

2. New Crowdfunding Law

A standalone "On Crowdfunding" bill would be Azerbaijan's first dedicated legal regime for crowdfunding platforms. Fintech and capital-markets clients exploring alternative-finance offerings should watch this closely through subsequent readings — it may define licensing, investor-protection, and platform-liability rules from scratch.

3. Preschool Financing Model

Cabinet Decision No. 218 introduces a parent–state–private co-financing model for preschool education — a notable shift from purely state-funded provision. Clients in private education or PPP advisory should review the full text once published on e-qanun.az for investment and licensing implications.

4. Order-Numbering Gap Worth Flagging

Cabinet Orders jump from No. 495s to Nos. 507s–510s within two days, with 496s–506s unaccounted for in the public listing. This is likely a publication-lag or partial-disclosure artifact rather than a substantive gap, but any client matter turning on a specific order in that range should be verified directly with the Cabinet's registry rather than assumed absent.
