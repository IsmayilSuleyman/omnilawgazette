Lead Story New Civil Service Code signed; implementing decree sets out who does what
On 16 November the President signed and published Law No. 490-VIIQ (adopted by the Milli Majlis on 16 October 2026) approving the Civil Service Code of the Republic of Azerbaijan. The Code replaces the framework that has governed public employment since 2000. On its entry into force it repeals six laws, among them the Law on Civil Service (2000, No. 926-IQ), the Law on Ethical Conduct Rules for Civil Servants (2007), the law on reduced working hours for some categories of civil servants, the law on the Civil Service Management Council, and the two laws on regional and hazardous-duty salary coefficients. Other acts stay in force insofar as they do not conflict with the Code until they are brought into line with it. Anyone holding a valid "Civil Service Certificate" from a test passed under the old law may still sit interviews under the Code until the certificate expires.

Decree No. 817 of the same day puts the law into effect. It names the authority behind each "relevant body" in the Code: the President, the Cabinet of Ministers, the Presidential Administration, the State Examination Centre and individual ministries. It repeals four implementing decrees from 2000–2007. It also gives the Cabinet five months to draft or approve a series of rules: the classification of administrative and support posts, the interview procedure, internal investigations, the test-exam and certificate procedure, medical examinations, pay for independent interview experts, trainee and further training. The Tariff (Price) Council has three months to set the fees for taking the test and the interview. HR and public-sector clients: expect a wave of secondary rules in Q1–Q2 2027.

Decrees (Fərmanlar)

DecreeNo. 821 16 Nov 2026
Caspian coastline: one body now runs land management
Makes the State Service for Property Issues (under the Ministry of Economy) responsible for state policy on managing, using and protecting land along Azerbaijan's Caspian coast. Islands, settlements, industrial, energy, port, defence and border areas, and other special-status zones are excluded. The Service gains 40 staff. Within six months the Cabinet must propose a special legal status and zoning regime for the coastal strip and draft tougher administrative and criminal liability for illegal land seizure. In the same period it must approve the strip's boundaries (on paper and in digital form) in agreement with the President. Leasing of coastal land is to run on a "one window" basis through the e-Government system, and a GIS-based information system is to be proposed.
DecreeNo. 819 16 Nov 2026
"Single Window" information system: Statute approved
Implements Decree No. 547 (15 Dec 2025) on digitalising import-export, transit, transport and logistics. It approves the Statute of the "Single Window" system, and the agencies that source its data have six months to upgrade their systems and integrate through the e-Government Information System. Relevant to trade, customs and logistics clients.
DecreeNo. 816 16 Nov 2026
Public procurement: AZCON designated
Implements Law No. 497-VIIQD. The Azerbaijan Transport and Communications Holding (AZCON) is the body named in new Articles 3.2.8-1 and 3.10-1 of the Law on Public Procurement, so AZCON approves the procurement rules for itself and the entities it manages.

Laws signed & published

LawNo. 502-VIIQD pub. 17 Nov
Central Bank Law: AML/CFT supervision rewritten
Article 48-1 is restated. The Central Bank acts as supervisor under the AML/CFT Law and the Law on Targeted Financial Sanctions. It runs periodic risk profiling of financial institutions and groups, may demand documents by written request, and sets how often and how closely it supervises according to risk. Groups that include banks, life insurers, reinsurers, insurance intermediaries, investment firms and funds are supervised on a consolidated basis.
LawNo. 499-VIIQD pub. 17 Nov
Labour Code & Aviation Law: flight crew hours
New Labour Code Articles 94-1 and 103-1, and Aviation Law Articles 1.1.82-1 ("flight duty period") and 27-1: crew working and rest time follows ICAO standards and Cabinet rules, with caps over any 7 and 14 consecutive days. The Labour Code fills any gaps.
LawNo. 504-VIIQD pub. 16 Nov
Labour Code: work stoppages in windy weather
Article 233 and Annex 2 now cover windy as well as cold and hot weather, including outdoor workplaces. Cranes may operate in winds of 6–8 Beaufort only where their technical passports allow it; above 8, all crane work stops.
LawNo. 497-VIIQD pub. 16 Nov
Public Procurement Law: carve-out for designated holding
The designated body (now AZCON; see Decree 816), its managed enterprises and the entities they hold stakes in buy under their own rules when spending budget funds, state-guaranteed loans, grants and similar money.

Practitioner Spotlight: What to Watch

1. Public employment reset

The Civil Service Code and Decree 817 replace a 25-year-old framework. Testing, interviews, internal investigations and ethics rules all move to new procedures due within roughly five months. Existing Civil Service Certificates stay valid until they expire.

2. Coastal land under one authority

Decree 821 hands Caspian-coast land administration to the State Service for Property Issues and signals tougher penalties for illegal seizure. Clients with coastal leases or development plans should watch the boundary maps and zoning regime due within six months.

3. AML/CFT supervision formalised

The rewritten Art. 48-1 of the Central Bank Law sets risk-based, consolidated AML/CFT supervision and adds the Targeted Financial Sanctions Law to the Central Bank's remit. Banks, insurers and investment firms should check their group-level compliance.

4. Budget season and a heavy 24 Nov agenda

The 2027 budget package carries large omnibus amendments to the Tax, Labour, Competition and insolvency laws. A stand-alone Conflict of Interest Law and the restated Absheron PSA are listed for 24 November.
